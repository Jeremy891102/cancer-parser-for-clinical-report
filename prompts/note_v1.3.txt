## 4. Diagnosis extraction and normalization

Separate distinct cancers, benign/borderline lesions and unresolved lesions when clinically different. Do not duplicate one diagnosis merely because it appears repeatedly in the note. Diagnosis granularity and exact quotations:

- diagnosis_as_documented: retain the relevant original diagnosis phrase or sentence, including negation, uncertainty and useful context. Clean whitespace only; do not rewrite it into a more certain diagnosis. Do not copy the whole note. A concise quoted span is sufficient; other diagnosis objects may preserve separate relevant statements.
- histologic_subtype: choose the most specific supported exact name in the 56-item reference. Map equivalent synonyms, abbreviations and word-order variants semantically; matching need not be verbatim. Examples: high grade serous adenocarcinoma -> High-grade serous carcinoma; carcinoma of ovary and ovarian carcinoma -> Carcinoma / cancer, subtype unspecified if no finer subtype is stated. Do not infer high grade from the word serous alone. Preserve explicitly documented mixed histology in diagnosis_as_documented; do not force an unsupported single subtype. For an unlisted tumor or unrepresented mixed subtype use "other: <concise tumor name>". For a non-neoplastic finding or absent subtype use null. In particular, endometriosis and ordinary non-neoplastic stromal hyperplasia have null subtype; reference inclusion does not make a non-neoplastic finding a tumor.
- diagnostic_qualification: certainty of the assigned subtype only: confirmed, uncertain or null. Qualified subtype wording such as favored, possible, suspicious for or in favor of means uncertain. Use confirmed when the subtype is stated definitively; use null if no subtype is assigned. This is not a generic cancer-presence or origin certainty field. A definitive adenocarcinoma with favored endometrioid differentiation has an uncertain endometrioid subtype, not uncertain existence of carcinoma.
- primary_origin_site: normalize documented origin using consistent organ/regional names, including non-target organs. Examples: ovary, fallopian_tube, peritoneum, uterus, breast, colon, lung, thyroid. Use uterus for an endometrial primary. Use peritoneum, not primary_peritoneum. Do not include laterality or finer sublocations. For a broad Mullerian origin without a specific organ use mullerian_unspecified with uncertain certainty. If no origin can be identified, use null. A sampling site or organ involvement alone is insufficient. A staging-template title, CA125 elevation, BRCA status or drug regimen alone does not establish a target primary. A definitive physician diagnosis such as ovarian cancer or ovarian adenocarcinoma supports ovary without requiring the literal word primary, unless contradicted or explicitly described as secondary from another organ. Distinguish 'ovarian cancer' from 'carcinoma involving the ovary'. Personal cancer history can establish that historical diagnosis's origin but does not automatically establish the origin of a separate new lesion.
- primary_origin_certainty: confirmed, uncertain or null. Confirmed means the note documents the origin definitively, not independent validation. Retain uncertainty for qualified attribution. If origin is wholly unknown, use null site and uncertain certainty when the note explicitly states unknown primary; if origin is simply unaddressed, both can be null. Do not turn 'most consistent with prior ovarian cancer' into an independently confirmed origin. A separately established history of ovarian cancer remains its own supported historical diagnosis.
- temporal_status: current_disease, history_only, remission_or_ned or uncertain. current_disease requires documented current malignancy, recurrence/metastasis or an explicitly current unresolved/benign/borderline lesion. history_only means a prior diagnosis/finding is documented without established current activity. remission_or_ned requires explicit remission/NED for that diagnosis. uncertain means timing/activity cannot be established. Treatment alone does not prove current disease; follow-up/surveillance alone does not prove NED. Stable documented metastatic disease is still current_disease. A dated historical NED scan alone does not establish current NED. When current activity remains unresolved, use uncertain. Old copied disease-history sections do not automatically override an explicit present assessment. Do not infer uninterrupted active cancer between historical events.

Date selection rules:
1. Prefer an explicit initial diagnosis date for the selected condition. Keep it in later recurrence, remission or NED notes.
2. If no explicit initial date is available, use a clearly linked initial diagnostic biopsy, pathology collection, surgery or imaging event as the available diagnosis-event date. 3. An explicitly stated month/year event can be refined to a complete date from a matching procedure record in the SAME note when the procedure, clinician and timeline clearly identify the same event. Do not refine it from an unrelated procedure.
4. If the only dated event is explicitly recurrence/metastasis of an already known condition and its initial date is unavailable, use null. Do not substitute the recurrence operation, metastatic biopsy, later treatment, routine surveillance scan or signing date for the unknown initial date.

Evaluate the linkage and source specificity; prefer the directly linked diagnostic event over an unlinked or less-specific summary. ## 6. One report-level study classification
Apply the reference WITH patient ownership, diagnosis certainty, primary origin and history. Reference group membership alone is not proof of target origin. An unlisted subtype can still be Malignant when target malignancy is established.

- Malignant: a supported current OR PREVIOUS target primary malignancy, including linked recurrence/metastasis. Explicit NED does not erase this classification for the ever-target endpoint. A malignant finding plus benign specimens remains Malignant. Preserve other primaries separately.
- Borderline: a supported target-site borderline tumor, without established target invasive malignancy taking precedence. Isolated STIC maps to Borderline under this STUDY-SPECIFIC rule; keep subtype STIC. This is not a clinical claim that STIC is histologically a borderline tumor. Do not extend this special rule to all unspecified in-situ diagnoses.
- Benign: a relevant lesion is explicitly diagnosed as benign, without established target malignancy or a relevant borderline tumor taking precedence. Silence about cancer is not Benign.
- Other: established malignancy with confirmed non-target primary, including its metastases, and no established target malignancy or unresolved target-related lesion taking precedence. Unknown primary is not Other. A current unresolved target-related malignancy/origin should not be dismissed as Other merely because a separate non-target cancer is known.
- Uncertain: target classification remains unresolved. This includes unestablished malignancy, unknown/broad Mullerian primary relevant to target mapping, unresolved tumor behavior, or no relevant patient cancer information. Family-history-only notes are Uncertain, not patient-positive. This does not necessarily imply clinical suspicion of cancer.

An unresolved ovarian/adnexal, fallopian-tube or peritoneal lesion takes priority over a separate established non-target cancer: select Uncertain rather than Other when target status remains unresolved. In that situation, diagnosis_date must relate to the unresolved target lesion, not to the non-target cancer. Keep all non-target diagnoses in diagnoses. A mere risk factor or family history is not itself an unresolved lesion.

Use Malignant when independently established target malignancy exists; do not downgrade it because a separate lesion is uncertain. Otherwise consider unresolved target-related findings before settling on Borderline, Benign or Other. Keep one category only. Do not add uncertainty subcategories. The unspecified ovarian carcinoma-in-situ entry remains unresolved for invasive-target classification unless other evidence or a specific study rule resolves it; do not silently classify it as invasive Malignant.
